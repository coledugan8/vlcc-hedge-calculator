VLCC Freight Hedging Calculator

An oil trader needs one Very Large Crude Carrier for the next 12 months. Should they lock in a 12-month time charter now (hedge) or stay on the spot market? This tool forecasts VLCC earnings from oil-market drivers and prices the trade-off.

⚠ Using the workbook's simulated data. Results show how the model works, not what the real market did. The live data file was not found (it appears after the first scheduled update runs).

Key terms

VLCC
Very Large Crude Carrier, a tanker carrying about 2 million barrels of crude.
TCE (time-charter equivalent)
A ship's voyage earnings converted to dollars per day so routes can be compared.
Time charter
Hiring a ship for a fixed period at a fixed daily rate. For a charterer this is the hedge: the cost is known in advance.
Spot market
Hiring ships voyage by voyage at whatever the market rate is at the time.
FFA (forward freight agreement)
A financial contract settled against a freight index such as TD3C (Middle East Gulf to China). Another way to lock in a freight rate.
Contango
When future oil prices exceed today's price, making it profitable to store oil at sea, which takes tankers out of service and pushes rates up.

Limitations

  • A linear model with three drivers. It ignores fleet supply, sanctions, OPEC+ decisions, seasonality and geopolitical shocks.
  • Only one-month-ahead accuracy is tested; 12-month forecasts are recursive and much less certain.
  • The uncertainty band assumes roughly normal errors; real freight shocks are fat-tailed.
  • The spot vs time-charter comparison ignores voyage costs, off-hire and counterparty risk.
  • The live freight series is the U.S. deep-sea freight producer price index, a free proxy covering all deep-sea shipping, not VLCCs alone, and scaled to $/day by assumption.
